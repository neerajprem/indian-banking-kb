## What a payment rail is

A payment rail is a shared system that banks use to move money for their customers. Each rail has an operator, a rulebook, a message format and a way of settling between banks. In India the main operators are the Reserve Bank of India (RBI), which runs RTGS and NEFT, and the National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI), which runs UPI, IMPS, NACH, the card and ATM network, and cheque clearing.

Every payment has two parts. The message tells the banks what to do. Settlement then moves the money between the banks themselves, in the accounts they hold at RBI.<Note>Banks hold current accounts with RBI. Moving money between those accounts is what settlement means on this page.</Note> This is why a customer can see a credit within seconds while the banks settle later.

## Gross and net settlement

Gross settlement moves each payment on its own, at once. It removes the risk that one bank's failure strands everyone else's money, but each bank needs enough money in its RBI account to cover every payment as it goes. Net settlement adds up what each bank owes and is owed over a period, then moves only the difference. That needs far less cash, but money stays in flight until the cycle closes, and a bank that cannot pay its net amount affects everyone in that cycle.

## IMPS and UPI

IMPS and UPI both credit the customer within seconds, at any hour, and both settle between banks later, on a net basis. IMPS came first and is used from bank apps and branches with an account number and IFSC. UPI added a simpler address, the UPI ID, and opened payments to apps that are not banks. In August 2026, IMPS handled <Fact id="imps-volume-2026-08" /> and UPI <Fact id="upi-volume-2026-08" />, about <Fact id="upi-daily-2026-08" />.

### Who takes part in a UPI payment

- **The payer's bank** holds the account that is debited. It checks the UPI PIN and the balance.
- **The app** is where the customer starts the payment. It is either the bank's own app or a third-party app provider (TPAP) that works through one or more partner banks, called payment service providers (PSPs).
- **NPCI** runs the switch that routes the request between banks and works out what each bank owes the others.
- **The payee's bank** credits the person or merchant being paid.

When a customer first sets up an app, UPI ties the app to that phone and its SIM, a step called device binding. Each payment is then approved with the UPI PIN. The PIN proves the customer; the binding proves the phone.

### Ways to pay with UPI

UPI has grown several variants on the same rails. AutoPay lets a customer approve a recurring payment, such as a subscription, once. UPI Lite keeps a small balance on the phone for quick, low-value payments without entering the PIN each time. UPI 123PAY brings UPI to feature phones through voice calls, missed calls and simple apps. NPCI publishes the current limits for each.

UPI is no longer only about bank balances. <Fact id="rupay-cc-upi-2022" show="claim" /> <Fact id="upi-credit-lines-2023" show="claim" />

## When a payment fails

A payment can end in three ways. It succeeds; it fails before any money moves; or it gets stuck, with the payer debited but the payee not credited. The third case is the one customers notice, and RBI sets rules for fixing it. <Fact id="tat-failed-definition" show="claim" /> <Fact id="tat-2019" show="claim" />

## Bulk and recurring payments: NACH

NACH moves many payments in one go, such as a company's salaries, dividends, or the monthly instalments a lender collects. Collections run under a mandate: the customer authorises a lender or biller once, and the bank then debits the account on each due date. Files are processed in batches, so NACH suits planned, scheduled money rather than urgent payments.

## Cards, tokens and ATMs

Card payments run over card networks, including NPCI's domestic network, RuPay. Online card payments changed in 2022. <Fact id="cof-2022" show="claim" /> A token stands in for the card number and works only for the merchant it was issued for, so a breached merchant database no longer exposes usable card numbers.

ATM withdrawals at another bank's machine travel over the National Financial Switch. <Fact id="nfs-npci-2009" show="claim" /> <Fact id="nfs-atms-2024" show="claim" />

Card networks must also keep payment data in India. <Fact id="data-localisation-2018" show="claim" /> <Fact id="amex-diners-2021" show="claim" /> <Fact id="mastercard-restriction-2021" show="claim" /> <Fact id="mastercard-lifted-2022" show="claim" />

## Bill payments: BBPS

The Bharat Bill Payment System (BBPS) lets customers pay electricity, water, telecom and many other bills through any participating bank, app or agent, with a common confirmation. Its value is interoperability: a biller connects once and can be paid through every channel on the system.

## Messaging networks: SFMS, INFINET and SWIFT

Behind RTGS and NEFT sit a private network and a messaging layer. <Fact id="infinet-1999" show="claim" /> <Fact id="sfms-2001" show="claim" /> <Fact id="iftas-hiveoff-2015" show="claim" /> For cross-border payments, banks use SWIFT, the global cooperative network for financial messages.

## Account Aggregators: moving data, not money

An Account Aggregator (AA) is not a payment rail, but lenders now use it alongside the rails, for example to check a borrower's bank statements with consent. <Fact id="aa-md-2016" show="claim" /> <Fact id="aa-consent" show="claim" />

Three roles make it work. A financial information provider (FIP), such as a bank, holds the data. A financial information user (FIU), such as a lender, asks for it. The aggregator carries the request and the encrypted data between them. <Fact id="aa-data-blind" show="claim" /> <Fact id="rebit-aa-specs" show="claim" />
