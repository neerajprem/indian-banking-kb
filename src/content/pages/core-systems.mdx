## What a core banking system is

A core banking system (CBS) holds a bank's customers, accounts and balances, and posts every debit and credit to the general ledger. It is the system of record: when two systems disagree about a balance, the core is right by definition.

"Core" also means centralised. Before core banking, each branch kept its own books, and a customer could only be served at the branch that held the account. A central core lets every branch, ATM and app see the same account at the same moment. That shift is what made anywhere banking and, later, instant payments possible.<Note>Instant rails such as UPI assume that any bank can debit or credit any account in seconds, at any hour. That is only possible because each bank's accounts sit in one central system.</Note>

## The layers around the core

A core rarely talks to customers directly. Channels reach it through an integration layer, and a ring of specialist systems sits around it.

## The daily cycle: end of day and beginning of day

A core does two kinds of work. Online processing handles each transaction as it happens: a withdrawal, a transfer, a balance enquiry. Batch processing handles work that is easier to do for every account at once, such as accruing interest, applying charges and producing statements.

The batch run that closes a business day is called end of day (EOD). The run that opens the next day is beginning of day (BOD).

### Why round-the-clock payments strain the core

Many cores were designed around a quiet night. Instant rails never have one: UPI alone now averages <Fact id="upi-daily-2026-08" />. So a bank cannot simply stop posting while its end-of-day batch runs.

Banks bridge the gap in a few ways. A stand-in layer keeps a shadow balance and approves or declines payments while the core is busy, then replays them afterwards. Batch jobs are split into smaller pieces that run while the core stays online. Some banks move payment processing into separate systems that post to the core in bulk. Each approach adds reconciliation work: two systems now hold a version of the balance, and they must agree.

## How cores are deployed

A common pattern is for a large bank to run its core in its own data centres, with a second site for disaster recovery (see [multi-data-centre and disaster recovery](/architecture/multi-dc/)). Smaller banks, especially in the cooperative sector, often use a shared platform instead of running their own. <Fact id="ibcc-cbs" show="claim" />

Running a core on a vendor's or a public cloud is possible, but it counts as outsourcing and brings RBI's rules for outsourced IT with it (see [hybrid and multi-cloud deployment](/architecture/hybrid-cloud/)). <Fact id="rbi-md-it-outsourcing-2023" show="claim" />

## Why cores are hard to change

A core accumulates decades of products, interest rules and integrations. Replacing it means moving every account, every balance and every open loan without losing a rupee, while the bank keeps trading. That is why core replacements are rare, run for years, and are usually done in stages rather than all at once.
