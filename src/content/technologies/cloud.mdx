## Why it matters

Cloud platforms give banks capacity on demand, managed databases and analytics, and environments that can be created in minutes. Whether and how a bank uses them is shaped less by technology than by rules: where data may live, what counts as outsourcing and how the bank would leave. [Hybrid and multi-cloud deployment](/architecture/hybrid-cloud/) covers the architecture; this page covers the providers and constraints.

## The regulatory frame

Three rules shape almost every cloud decision:

- **Data residency.** <Fact id="data-localisation-2018" show="claim" />
- **Outsourcing.** <Fact id="outsourcing-cloud-scope" show="claim" /> <Fact id="outsourcing-cloud-shared" show="claim" />
- **Exit.** <Fact id="outsourcing-exit" show="claim" />

### Sovereign and community cloud

Some workloads are better served by cloud infrastructure dedicated to a sector or a country. A **community cloud** is shared among a defined group, such as banks of one type. <Fact id="ibcc-cbs" show="claim" /> A **sovereign cloud** is operated so that data, operations and control stay within one jurisdiction. Both trade some of the public cloud's breadth for tighter control.

## How to choose

Most banks choose a provider per workload rather than for the whole estate. The deciding factors are usually:
- the software the workload already depends on;
- the services it needs, such as a managed database, analytics or AI;
- the availability of two regions in India for resilience;
- the bank's existing skills and contracts;
- how easily the workload could move elsewhere.

Start with workloads that benefit most and carry least risk, such as development environments, analytics on masked data and customer-facing channels. Build the controls, contracts and evidence once, then reuse them.

## Pitfalls

- **Assuming the provider handles security.** Configuration, identity and data protection remain the bank's job.
- **Data leaving India unnoticed,** through backups, logs, support access or a global service.
- **Lock-in by convenience,** when a workload uses so many provider-specific services that it cannot move.
- **Cost surprises** from idle resources and data transfer.
- **An exit plan written only for the auditor.**

## Questions for a design review

- In which regions will this workload's data, backups and logs reside?
- Which controls does the bank own, and which does the provider?
- What provider-specific services does this workload use, and what would replace them on exit?
- Has the outsourcing assessment been completed and approved?
- Who owns the running cost, and how is it reviewed?
