## Why it matters

A payment passes through an app, a gateway, several services, a payment switch and a core. When customers report failures, the team must find which step is failing, for whom and since when, in minutes. Observability is the ability to answer those questions from the data systems emit, without guessing or adding code during the incident.

## Three kinds of signal

**Metrics** are numbers over time: requests per second, error rate, response time, queue depth. They are cheap to store and ideal for dashboards and alerts. **Logs** are records of individual events, with detail about what happened. They are essential for investigation and audit, but costly at volume. **Traces** follow one request across every service it touches, showing where time was spent and where it failed. Together they answer "is something wrong?", "what happened?" and "where?".

## From data to decisions: SLOs

A **service level indicator** (SLI) is a measure of what users experience, such as the share of payments that succeed within two seconds. A **service level objective** (SLO) is the target for it, such as 99.9% over 30 days. The gap between the target and perfection is the **error budget**: while it lasts, the team can ship changes; when it runs out, reliability work comes first.

## Alerting, on-call and incident review

Good alerts fire on symptoms users would notice, such as rising payment failures, rather than on every internal blip. Each alert has an owner, a runbook and a clear severity. On-call rotations share the load fairly and include time to recover. After every significant incident, a **blameless review** records what happened, why it made sense to the people involved at the time and what will change. ## How to choose

Instrument once with OpenTelemetry, so the choice of back end can change later. Start with the few SLIs that matter most to customers, such as payment success and response time, and build dashboards and alerts around them before adding more. Decide log retention by need and rule, not by default.

## Pitfalls

- **Dashboards nobody watches** and alerts nobody owns.
- **Alerting on causes,** such as high CPU, instead of symptoms users feel.
- **Personal data in logs,** such as full account numbers or card details.
- **Broken traces** because one service does not pass the trace ID on.
- **Reviews that assign blame,** which teach people to hide problems.

## Questions for a design review

- What are this service's SLIs and SLOs, and who agreed them?
- If payments start failing, which alert fires first, and who receives it?
- Can one payment be traced from the app to the core?
- What personal data could appear in these logs, and how is it masked?
- How long are logs kept, and why that long?
