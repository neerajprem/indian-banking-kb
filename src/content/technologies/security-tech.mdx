## Why it matters

No single product makes a bank secure. Each tool covers a different way an attacker could get in or a different stage of an attack: preventing, detecting or responding. [Security architecture](/architecture/security/) explains how the layers fit together; this page describes the technology families.

This page is conceptual. It does not describe any organisation's tools or configuration.

## Where banking adds requirements

- **Cards and keys.** Card numbers are replaced by tokens wherever possible, shrinking what must be protected: <Fact id="cof-2022" show="claim" /> PINs and keys are handled inside HSMs.
- **Customer authentication.** <Fact id="auth-2025" show="claim" />
- **Digital certificates.** <Fact id="idrbt-ca" show="claim" />
- **Reporting clocks.** Detection tools must feed a process fast enough to meet deadlines. <Fact id="certin-6h-2022" show="claim" /> <Fact id="outsourcing-6h" show="claim" />

## How to choose

Choose by the risk each tool reduces, in order of what would hurt most: money movement, keys, customer data and administrator access. Prefer fewer tools that are well integrated and fully used over many that are partly deployed. For each tool, decide in advance who owns it, who reads its alerts and what happens when it fires.

## Pitfalls

- **Shelfware:** tools bought, partly installed and never tuned.
- **Alert fatigue,** where so many alerts fire that real ones are missed.
- **Blind spots,** such as cloud accounts, supplier connections or new services not sending logs.
- **Firewall rule sprawl,** with thousands of rules nobody can explain.
- **Secrets outside the vault,** in scripts, tickets or chat.

## Questions for a design review

- Which risk does each security tool here reduce, and who owns it?
- Does every system, including cloud and supplier connections, send logs to the SIEM?
- Who reads alerts at 3 a.m., and how fast can an incident reach the reporting deadline?
- Which administrator accounts have standing access, and why?
- Where are this system's secrets, and when were they last rotated?
