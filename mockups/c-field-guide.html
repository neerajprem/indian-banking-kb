Follow one UPI payment

Select a step, or use Next, to see who does what when you pay someone by UPI.

  1. You enter the payee's UPI ID or scan their QR code, type the amount and confirm with your UPI PIN.

  2. The app passes the request, through the bank it works with, to NPCI's UPI system.

  3. NPCI checks which bank holds the account behind the payee's UPI ID.

  4. NPCI asks your bank to debit your account. Your bank checks your UPI PIN and balance, debits you and replies.

  5. NPCI asks the payee's bank to credit the payee, and the bank confirms.

  6. NPCI confirms to your app, and the payee's bank notifies the payee. This usually takes seconds.

  7. The banks settle what they owe each other, net, in their accounts at the Reserve Bank of India.

Simplified and illustrative. Real flows also involve the app providers and the banks they work with.

Gross versus net settlement

Every payment has two parts. The message tells the banks what to do. Settlement then moves the money between the banks themselves, in the accounts they hold at the Reserve Bank of India (RBI).1 Banks hold current accounts with RBI. Moving money between those accounts is what “settlement” means here. This is why a customer can see a credit within seconds while the banks settle later.

The customer sees the credit in seconds. The banks settle later.

Gross settlement moves each payment on its own. It removes the risk that one bank's failure strands everyone else's money, but it needs a lot of cash on hand. Net settlement adds up what each bank owes and is owed over a period, then moves only the difference. That saves cash, but money stays in flight until the cycle closes. The cheque system now settles this way every hour, on realisation.2 Realisation here means the paying bank has confirmed the cheque, or the confirmation window closed without a reply.

Changed recently

Cheques no longer wait until the next working day. Under RBI's circular of 13 August 2025, the cheque system moved from batch processing to continuous clearing, with settlement every hour on realisation, from 4 October 2025.1
